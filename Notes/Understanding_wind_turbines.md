* specific power = generator rating/ blade size (area swept)
* The blade must be light, stiff and fatigue resistant because gravity and wind bend it millions of times
* A turbine blade is a large lightweight cantilever beam
* Interior is hollow, spar cap is the structural skeleton to handle bending, shear webs is an internal structure component acting as the vertical, load bearing plate between the upper and lower spar caps
* Advanced polymer matrix composites, fibers embedded in resin, to achieve high strength to weight ratios, durability and corrosion resistance. it is made of Glass FIber Reinforced Plastics and Carbon Fiber reinforced Plastics
* Blades of a wind turbine are affected by 4 forces, drag, lift, centrifugal and gravitational
* Drag force is in parallel to the blade, whereas the lift is perpendicular. The pressure difference across the curved surfaces drives rotation, just like an aircraft wing
* The blade is a bit twisted to that the final velocity vector attacks the cross section at the optimal angle of attack
* Bending Moment is highest at the root, hence it needs to be structurally strong
* lift being stronger than drag causes the blade to spin
* Simultaneously, the lift force on the blades generates torque and has an equal but opposite effect on the wind, tending to push the air tangentially in the other direction — this is why the air downwind of the turbine has "swirl" spinning opposite to the blades. That swirl represents lost energy, which is one reason BEM theory needs a tangential induction correction (very important to understand)
* Due to this wake rotation, it makes it impossible to reach the Betz limit of efficiency
* **Understand what is BEM theory**
* The blade has more material with very high strength near the hub because of the higher stresses in that region, and less material near the narrow tip. For this reason, lift is increased and drag is reduced near the tip
* blade failure is mainly due to fatiguing over time. other causes also include lightning strikes, material delamination, erosion from rain and sand, overload from storms (high speed winds). Also caused by cyclic loading, vibrations and fatigue accumulation
* The Betz Limit, or Betz Law, states that no wind turbine can convert more than 59.3% (\\(16/27\\)) of the wind’s kinetic energy into electrical energy. Established by German physicist Albert Betz in 1919, this theoretical maximum indicates that even a perfect wind turbine cannot capture all the energy from the wind.
